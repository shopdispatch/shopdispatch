Fee and No-Slot Scripts

ShopDispatch · word-for-word lines for the hardest moments on daytime and after-hours calls

A few moments decide a lot of calls, day or night: saying the fee, answering “how much?”, and saying you don’t have a slot. Here are the exact lines. Fill the blanks from your shop’s written rules before the phones open — don’t make up policy on the call.

Fill these in first

  • Daytime trip / diagnostic fee: exact amount, and what it covers (for example: getting a tech out, finding the problem, a written price before any work starts).
  • After-hours / emergency fee: exact amount or formula, and when it applies.
  • For each fee: is it credited toward the repair if they go ahead? Yes or no. Write it down so everyone says it the same way.
  • Replacement range, only if your shop has decided on one in writing. If not, leave it out.
  • One named person who can authorize a book tonight.
  • Where real capacity lives tonight (board, sheet, calendar).
  • Escalate list: triggers (gas smell, no heat with medical risk, electrical fire risk, active flooding) and who gets the handoff.

If the fee or the booker is blank, don’t promise a truck. Take the details and escalate.

If your shop does free inspections or estimates, skip the fee lines and say that instead.

1. Stating the fee — once, up front

Say the fee once, before any truck promise, with what it covers and whether it comes off the bill. Pick the credited or not-credited sentence from your written rules and use it every time.

Daytime trip / diagnostic fee:

“Our tech will diagnose it and give you a written price before any work starts. There’s a [daytime fee] trip fee that covers [what it covers]. [If credited:] It comes off the bill if you go ahead with the repair. [If not credited:] It covers the diagnosis on its own, separate from any repair. I have [time] or [time]. Which works?”

After hours:

“Because this is after hours, our dispatch fee is [after-hours fee]. That covers [what it covers]. [If credited:] It comes off the bill if you go ahead with the repair. [If not credited:] It covers the visit on its own, separate from any repair. Are you okay with that fee if we can get you a real slot?”

  • Yes: log fee stated and acknowledged. Move on to checking capacity.
  • They push back: acknowledge once, say what it covers in one sentence, and offer a time. “I understand. That fee gets a tech out and covers finding the problem, and you get a written price before any work starts. I have [time]. Want it?” Don’t repeat the fee louder.
  • Still no: don’t argue. “Understood. The next option I can offer is [next real option: a regular-hours slot, or a later time today]. Want me to put you down for it?” If nothing books, log declined_after_fee and reason E_fee_declined.
  • You already talked about a truck and forgot the fee: stop. “Before we go further, I should have said this first: the fee is [fee], and it covers [what it covers]. Still want the visit?” Never leave the fee for the tech at the door.

2. When they want a price first

Never answer with “we don’t quote over the phone” and nothing else. Ask first, then give what you honestly can, then offer a time. Keep your tone easy — the question is fair.

Repair calls:

“Happy to help with pricing. Can I ask a couple of quick questions so I don’t guess wrong?”

  1. “What’s it doing right now?”
  2. “Do you know what kind of system it is, or about how old?”
  3. “When did it start?”

“Thanks. I can’t give you an honest repair price until a tech sees it — a phone guess could be wrong either way. What I can tell you is the visit: [fee line from section 1, with what it covers and credited yes/no]. I have [time] or [time]. Which works?”

Replacement shoppers (pricing a new system):

“I can give you a rough idea. So I don’t guess wrong, can I ask a few quick questions about your home?”

  1. “What’s going on with the system you have now?”
  2. “Do you know what kind it is, or about how old?”
  3. “About how big is the home, and how many floors?”

[Only if your written rules include a range:] “For a home like that, replacements usually fall somewhere in [range from your written rules]. That’s a wide range on purpose. The real number depends on what we see in your home and the options you pick, and you get a written estimate after that look. Can I set up an in-home estimate visit? I have [time] or [time]. [Estimate visit: free, or fee per your written rules.]”

No range in your written rules? Skip it. Don’t guess. “The real number depends on a look at your home, and I’d rather not guess wrong. Can I set up an in-home estimate visit?”

If they still won’t go ahead without a price, log reason F_wanted_price_first.

Not legal advice. Fee disclosure rules vary by state. Check with your state contractor board or a lawyer before you settle your fee wording.

3. Saying there’s no slot — honestly

Only confirm a window after you (or the named booker) check real capacity. Stale, missing, or conflicting capacity means no verified slot.

If there’s a real slot:

“I have a real window of [window the shop can keep]. I’ll put you in for that. You’ll get [callback / text] confirming. What’s the best number if the tech needs anything?”

If there isn’t:

“I don’t have a verified open slot I can promise right now, and I won’t fake a hold. Here’s what I can do: [next real option / first-thing callback / on-call lead]. I’m logging this so dispatch sees it first thing.”

Log booked = N, no_verified_slot, reason G_no_slot_that_worked. G is usually a capacity decision, so it’s the owner’s to look at on Friday, not the phone person’s fault.

If they’d rather wait for regular hours and nothing gets booked: reason H_calling_back, note “wants regular hours.” If a morning slot does go on the board, it’s booked and gets no code.

4. Escalating

“This needs a named person on our side right away. I’m contacting [name/role] now with your address, the problem, the fee, and what we’ve agreed. You’ll hear from [them / us] at [callback number].”

Hand off the full packet: address, issue, fee status, what the caller agreed to, callback number. The caller should know someone is on it, and who.

Short closes

  • Booked: “You’re set for [window]. Fee noted. We’ll use [number] if anything changes.”
  • Not booked: “Logged. No fake hold. Next step is [X].”
  • Escalated: “Handed to [name]. Stay by [number].”

Never promise on the call

  • A truck, ETA, or “hold” without a checked capacity row.
  • A diagnosis, parts availability, or a final repair price.
  • Anything outside your written fee and escalate rules.

Log each call in the Night-Call Log or circle it on the Reason Card. The full six moves are on the After-Hours Intake Checklist.
