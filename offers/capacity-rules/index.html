Weekly Capacity Rules

ShopDispatch · one page · keep it by the schedule

“No slot” is a schedule you set, not a phone problem. Write your capacity rules down once, so everyone who books and dispatches works from the same page. One page, no software required.

Download the sheet (PDF, letter size)

How to use it

  1. Fill it in once. Pick the days and how many slots you’ll hold per tech, and the time unused held slots get released.
  2. Write down who can book into a held slot, what moves first when an emergency comes in, and who calls the customer who gets moved.
  3. Name one owner for the callback list. Name one or two referral partners you’d send your own family to.
  4. Keep it by the schedule. On Friday, count your G calls by weekday and job type, change one thing, and compare two weeks before and after.

What’s on it

  • Held slots. Slots per tech, which days, who can take them, and whether online booking can.
  • Release time. When an unused held slot goes to the callback list. Pick a time and stick with it for a few weeks. We don’t know of a right answer.
  • What moves first. Routine work moves, not the new emergency. After hours, on-call is for emergency job types only.
  • Callback list. One named owner, where the list lives, and what to write down.
  • Referral partner. Two blanks for shops you trust, for the calls you truly can’t take.
  • Friday check. G by weekday and job type.

Holding one slot per tech, the callback list, and referring out are our suggestions, not data, and we can’t promise any result. ServiceTitan’s help doc gives one vendor example of capping maintenance job types at 75% to leave 25% for emergency calls. That’s their example, not a measured result, and your shop may look nothing like it. Not legal advice: check with your state contractor board or a lawyer about licensing and referral rules before you refer or subcontract work.
