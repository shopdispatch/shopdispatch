After-Hours Intake Checklist

ShopDispatch · tied to Issue #1 (emergency speed premium)

Use this tonight. Print it, stick it by the board, or hand it to whoever covers overflow. No software required.

Why this exists

Emergency homeowners aren’t shopping for the cheapest quote—they’re shopping for the shop that answers and books. After-hours and overflow are where conversion historically dies: the call gets answered (or doesn’t), the fee isn’t said, the slot isn’t real, and the premium job walks to the next name on the list.

This checklist turns “someone left a voicemail” into “we have enough to book or escalate.” It’s the same six moves that matter whether a human CSR or a future AI intake path is on the line.

Before the phone rings (set these once)

  • After-hours fee is written down — exact amount or formula, and when it applies. Surprises kill closes.
  • Who can authorize a book — name/role with booking authority after 6 p.m., weekends, and holidays.
  • True capacity rules — skills, zones, and open slots that are real for tonight/this weekend (not “someone will call you”).
  • Escalate list — gas smell, active flooding, no heat with medical vulnerability, electrical fire risk, roof compromise with weather in. Who gets the handoff and how.
  • Voicemail / overflow path is tested — call yourself after hours once. Confirm it rings where you think it does.

The six intake moves (qualify → book or escalate)

Use these in order on every after-hours or overflow call. Missing any one is how jobs burn.

1. Answer on overflow, nights, and weekends

  • First ring (or first human/AI path) is covered when the office is closed—not only when the day is calm.
  • Caller hears a shop, not a dead voicemail box with no next step.

2. Qualify fast

Capture, at minimum:

  • Service address (and access notes: gate code, dogs, unit #)
  • Job type / trade (HVAC / plumbing / electrical / roofing — and what failed)
  • Urgency (no heat, active leak, no power, weather exposure, etc.)
  • Callback number that works tonight
  • Who will be on site and when they can meet the tech

3. Say the fee when after-hours or emergency dispatch applies

  • State the after-hours / emergency fee before promising a truck.
  • Confirm they still want the visit after hearing the fee.
  • Note fee acknowledgment on the ticket so the tech isn’t the one breaking the news.

4. Book into real capacity

  • Match skills + zone + open slot that actually exists.
  • Give a window or ETA the shop can keep—not a hope.
  • If nothing is open: say so, offer the next real option, or escalate—don’t fake a hold.

5. Escalate true emergencies and edge cases

  • Use the escalate list from “Before the phone rings.”
  • Hand off to a named human with: address, issue, fee status, and what the caller already agreed to.
  • Caller knows someone is on it—and who.

6. Log the outcome

  • Booked / not booked / escalated — and why if it bounced.
  • Enough detail to coach tomorrow (fee surprise? wrong trade? no capacity? hung up?).
  • Ticket lands where dispatch will see it in the morning without a scavenger hunt.

Missing-info traps that burn jobs

Quick scan after each call—if any box is empty, you’re gambling the premium:

Gap What usually happens
No address / bad access notesTech can’t find the job; ETA slips; homeowner re-calls the next shop
Urgency never clarifiedYou dispatch light for a flood—or over-dispatch for a non-emergency
Fee not stated up frontClose dies at the door; chargebacks and one-star reviews
Slot isn’t real capacity“Someone will call you” = they call someone else
No escalate pathEdge cases sit in a queue while the house gets worse
No logYou can’t fix the pattern; same miss repeats next weekend

10-minute shop drill (do this once this week)

  1. Call your own after-hours line. Time to first useful response.
  2. Run one fake no-heat / active-leak scenario against this checklist.
  3. Mark which of the six moves broke.
  4. Fix the broken move before peak season does it for you.
